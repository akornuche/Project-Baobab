# Business Startup Checklist

## Quick Answer

Starting a business in Nigeria typically costs **₦100,000-200,000** and takes **14-30 days** for the minimum legal requirements. The exact cost depends on your business structure, share capital, and whether you use professional services.

## Overview

This checklist covers the essential steps to legally start and operate a business in Nigeria. Following these steps ensures you comply with all regulatory requirements from day one.

**Estimated Timeline:** 14-30 days  
**Estimated Cost:** ₦100,000-200,000 (minimum)

## Understanding the Basics

### Business Structures in Nigeria
1. **Sole Proprietorship (Business Name)** - Simplest, unlimited liability
2. **Partnership** - Two or more owners, shared liability
3. **Limited Liability Company (Ltd)** - Separate legal entity, limited liability
4. **Limited Partnership (LP)** - Mix of general and limited partners

### Why Plan Ahead?
Proper planning helps you:
- Avoid regulatory penalties
- Save money on avoidable fees
- Set up your business correctly from the start
- Prepare for future growth and funding

## Requirements Checklist

### Pre-Registration
- ✅ Choose business name and structure
- ✅ Prepare business plan
- ✅ Secure initial capital
- ✅ Identify business location

### Registration Steps
- ✅ Name search and reservation (CAC)
- ✅ Business registration (CAC)
- ✅ Obtain TIN (FIRS)
- ✅ Open business bank account
- ✅ Register for VAT (if applicable)
- ✅ Register for PAYE (if hiring employees)

### Post-Registration
- ✅ Display registration certificate publicly
- ✅ Register with state business development agency
- ✅ Obtain local government business permit
- ✅ Set up accounting system

## Timeline & Steps

### Week 1: Research & Preparation
- Day 1-2: Research business structure and name
- Day 3-4: Prepare business plan and capital
- Day 5: Finalize business location and layout

### Week 2: Registration
- Day 1-2: Submit name search to CAC
- Day 3-4: Complete and submit business registration forms
- Day 5: Pay registration fees and submit documents

### Week 3: Post-Registration
- Day 1-2: Collect CAC certificate
- Day 3: Apply for TIN at FIRS
- Day 4: Open business bank account
- Day 5: Register for VAT (if applicable)

### Week 4: Operational Setup
- Day 1-2: Obtain local government business permit
- Day 3: Register with relevant industry regulators
- Day 4-5: Set up accounting and record-keeping systems

## The Regulatory Picture

### Key Regulatory Bodies
1. **Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)** - Business registration
2. **Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS)** - Tax registration (TIN, VAT, PAYE)
3. **Local Government** - Business permit
4. **State Business Development Office (SBDO)** - SME support

### Annual Obligations
- File annual returns with CAC (Business Name: ₦50,000; Ltd: ₦150,000+)
- File tax returns with FIRS
- Renew business permit with local government
- Submit audited accounts (for Ltd companies)

## Common Mistakes

1. **Skipping the business plan** - A proper plan helps secure funding and avoid costly mistakes

2. **Underestimating costs** - Budget for at least 3 months of operating capital

3. **Using home address for registration** - Many states require a commercial address

4. **Not separating personal and business finances** - Keep separate bank accounts

5. **Ignoring tax obligations** - Register for all applicable taxes immediately

## What Happens Next

After completing your business registration, focus on:

1. **Marketing and customer acquisition**
2. **Building your team** (if hiring)
3. **Setting up operations**
4. **Financial management and record-keeping**
5. **Compliance and renewal scheduling**
